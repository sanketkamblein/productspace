Project Journey

1.5 years from Joule experiment to Sapphire GTM

Phase 1 · Discovery → SAP TechEd 2025

AR Agent — Joule chat, Private Cloud

Validated the core use case: ask Joule to analyse a customer account, surface overdue amounts and risk — no screen switching. Key friction: users wanted to act on results inside Joule, but chat had no structured way to surface inline actions. That seeded the Engagement Layer.

SAP TechEd 2025 ✓Customer Engagement
Phase 2 · Engagement Layer concept

Multi-agent orchestration · Public Cloud joins

Expanded from one agent to an AR Assistant orchestrating many. Designed the EL structure: Overview, Spaces, Jobs, Conversations — a composite UI where agents surface recommendations and specialists act without deep SAP knowledge. 13 agents across Collections, Disputes, and AR Accounting.

EL Architecture13 AgentsCollection Specialist focus
Phase 3 · User Validation + AR Accountant scope

Validated flows, dual-track delivery

Sessions with SAP and DHL practitioners confirmed: action recommendations over data dumps, 360° view as highest value, post-call documentation as the real time drain. Engineering built a Joule-only scaffold — design ran the richer EL target state in parallel for early demos.

User Validation ✓SAP + DHLAction-first UX

Learnings

What 1.5 years at the frontier taught me

Action-first, not insight-first

Early designs surfaced lots of data. User validation showed specialists wanted recommended actions — not more to process. EL had to lead with "what to do next."

Agents need explainability to earn trust

Specialists were skeptical of AI prioritisation until they saw the signals behind it. Visible reasoning ("prioritised: broken promises + overdue amount") was the turning point.

Cross-assistant integration is the hardest UX problem

The AR ↔ Billing handoff for dispute resolution required careful design of context transfer and HITL confirmation. Invisible orchestration breaks user mental models.

Platform constraints must reach UX in week one

LLM context limits in Private Cloud forced architecture decisions that directly affected UX — especially Smart Clearing. Finding out late means redesigning under pressure.
